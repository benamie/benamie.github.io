## About
{: #about}

I am an applied microeconomist who studies how financial and policy instruments--as well as the informational infrastructure behind them--shape producer behavior, environmental outcomes, and the distribution of risk. I combine causal inference, field and natural experiments, and Earth-observation data to examine how novel sources of information improve public and private decisions, as well as how programs can be designed around their limitations.

My current work spans three strands: (1) how the data and contract design behind agricultural index insurance affect its value to policyholders and the public, as well as how enrollment responds to seasonal forecasts; (2) how public and private capital can finance the transition to sustainable land use, from evaluating a $54 million randomized conservation-payment program to assessing how conservation practices are capitalized into farmland values; and (3) how firms respond to information about regulatory compliance risk. Throughout, I work with researchers across disciplines, often partnering with the institutions responsible for deploying capital or governing market participation, including insurers, lenders, and government agencies.

Institutionally, I'm an Assistant Professor at Virginia Tech in the [Agricultural and Applied Economics Department](https://aaec.vt.edu), an [ISCE Scholar](https://www.isce.vt.edu/outcomes-impact/faculty-highlights/2026-27-isce-scholars.html) with Virginia Tech’s [Institute for Society, Culture and Environment](https://isce.vt.edu/), and a faculty affiliate of the [VT Remote Sensing Graduate Education Group](https://rsigep.frec.vt.edu/), the [Global Change Center](https://www.globalchange.vt.edu/), and the [Stanford RegLab](https://reglab.stanford.edu/). I co-lead the Agricultural Insurance & Finance impact area for [NASA Harvest](https://nasaharvest.org/) and and RAAPID [https://www.raapid.org/](Rapid Agricultural Assessments for Policy Impact and Decision-Making), a NASA Harvest spin-out focused on anticipating and responding to food crises. I also serve on the [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/) State of the Profession Panel for the [2028–2037 Decadal Survey for Earth Science and Applications from Space](https://www.nationalacademies.org/our-work/decadal-survey-for-earth-science-and-applications-from-space-2028-2037) (through December 2027) and on the [Taylor Geospatial Institute](https://taylorgeospatial.org/)’s [Fields of the World](https://fieldsofthe.world/) User Advisory Group.

Previously, I was a postdoc in the [Markets, Risk, and Resilience (MRR) Innovation Lab](https://basis.ucdavis.edu/) in the Agricultural and Resource Economics department at UC Davis, following my Ph.D. from the [Emmett Interdisciplinary Program in Environment and Resources (E-IPER)](https://pangea.stanford.edu/eiper) at Stanford. Before grad school I was a research analyst at [Climate Policy Initiative](https://climatepolicyinitiative.org/), after majoring in economics, with a minor in environmental studies, at UNC-Chapel Hill.

## Teaching
{: #teaching}

Full course descriptions, syllabi, and guidance for students (including requesting reference letters) are on my **[Teaching & Mentoring](/teaching-and-mentoring/)** page.

- **Remote Sensing in the Social Sciences** — graduate course, instructor of record (Fall 2021, 2022, 2024, 2026). How remotely sensed data is and can be appropriately used in social science research, with a focus on readings in environment, agriculture, and economic development and hands-on lab exercises with cloud-based geospatial platforms. 
- **Climate Risk Management** — upper-level undergraduate course, instructor of record (Fall 2023). The science and economics of extreme weather and climate change; how we detect shifting weather patterns, their implications for society, and policy options to manage the risks.
- **Environmental Economic Analysis & Management** (AAEC 4314) — upper-level undergraduate course, instructor of record (Spring 2027, new). Quantitative methods and computer-aided tools for analyzing environmental problems and evaluating management and policy options; the capstone analysis course for the Environmental Economics minor.
- **Environmental & Sustainable Development Economics** — undergraduate course, instructor of record (Spring 2021, 2022). How approaches from economics can help society weigh policy questions relating to pollution and sustainable development; how environmental problems and policies vary between developing and developed country contexts. 

**Prior experience**

- **The Economics of Index Insurance** — co-instructor, short course for remote-sensing specialists (Nairobi, Kenya, Summer 2019). An applied short course on the design, pricing, and evaluation of agricultural index insurance for managing risk - for pracitioners.
- **World Food Economy** (ECON/ESS 106/206) — teaching assistant, Stanford University (undergraduate and graduate). The economics of global food production, agricultural markets, hunger, and food and environmental policy.
- **Environmental Governance** (ENVRES 250) — teaching assistant, Stanford University (undergraduate). Seminar on governing shared, common-pool resources (fisheries, forests, and water), and the human behaviors and institutions that shape environmental outcomes in socio-environmental systems. 
- **Energy in Transition: De-Carbonizing America** — teaching assistant, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (honors undergraduate seminar). The policies, technologies, and economics of the U.S. transition to lower-carbon energy.

## HECTARE Lab
{: #team}

The **HECTARE Lab** (Human Environment CompuTing and Agricultural REsearch Lab) brings together students and scholars engaged at the intersection of economics and data science related to issues affecting humans, agriculture, and the environment. We meet ~weekly during the academic year to present and get feedback on ongoing research as well as discuss issues and skills of common interest.
